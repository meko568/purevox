How to remove background music from a video

Three free ways to strip music out of a clip and keep the dialogue — no uploads, no watermarks, no subscriptions.

Background music ruins clips where the talking matters: lecture recordings, interviews, gameplay commentary, anime episodes with a loud OST buried under the voices. Most "audio remover" sites upload your file to a server, charge a subscription, or stamp a watermark on the result. Here's how to do it properly — for free, with everything running on your own machine.

Option 1 — PureVox Desktop (easiest)

  1. Download PureVox for Windows, macOS (Intel or Apple Silicon) or Linux.
  2. Open the app and drop your video in — MP4, MOV, MKV or any format your system can play.
  3. Start the job. An AI model called Demucs splits the audio into three stems: voice, music and effects.
  4. When it finishes, you get your video back with the music gone and the dialogue untouched.

Everything happens locally — your footage never leaves your disk. Long files are processed in chunks, and if your laptop dies mid-job you can resume where it left off.

Option 2 — the CLI (for terminals and old machines)

If you live in a terminal or your machine barely meets the requirements, the purevox CLI does the same job with a single command and a lighter footprint:

purevox -i input_video.mp4 -o output.mp4

Grab it from the command line download section — works on Linux and macOS, and on Windows through WSL.

Option 3 — Google Colab (no install, works on phones)

No computer powerful enough? Run PureVox on Google's hardware for free:

  1. Open the Colab notebook and sign in with a Google account.
  2. Run each cell top to bottom (Runtime → Run all).
  3. Upload your video when prompted, then wait for the cleaned file to download automatically.

Tips for the cleanest result

  • Dialogue louder than music works best. AI separation is good, but nothing recovers a voice that's buried 20 dB under an OST.
  • Expect near-perfect on speech-heavy audio — podcasts, lectures, commentary. Dense, vocal-heavy music is the hardest case for any separator.
  • Keep the original file. If the first pass isn't clean enough, re-run it rather than re-processing an already-processed export.
PureVox removes music and keeps the voice. If you need the opposite — instrumental only — this tool isn't built for that (yet).
